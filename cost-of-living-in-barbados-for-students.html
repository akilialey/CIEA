What It Costs to Study and Live in Barbados

A clear, sourced look at rent, food, transport, tuition and the one-off costs to plan for before you arrive.

Patricia AtherleyFounder & Director, CIEA

13 November 2026·7 min read

One of the first questions families ask me is simple: what will this really cost? It is the right question to start with. A year in Barbados is very achievable when you plan for it, and the best plans are built on real numbers. Here are the figures the schools and the Government of Barbados publish themselves, so you can build your own budget with confidence.

First, the Barbados dollar

Prices on the island are in Barbados dollars (BBD, often written BDS$). The Barbados dollar has been fixed to the US dollar at US$1 to BBD$2 since 5 July 1975, according to the Central Bank of Barbados. That makes the maths easy: halve a Barbados price to get the US dollar amount. A BBD$3.50 bus fare is US$1.75.

A monthly budget from UWI Cave Hill

The International Office at The University of the West Indies, Cave Hill Campus publishes estimated living costs for international students. These are their figures, in US dollars:

Estimated monthly living costs, UWI Cave Hill (US$)
ItemEstimate per month
Accommodation (shared)$300 to $400
Meals ($15 to $30 a day)$450 to $900
Daily transportation$100 to $200
Incidentals$200

Added together, those ranges come to roughly US$1,050 to US$1,700 a month. That total is my own sum of UWI's figures, not a number the university publishes. UWI also notes that a room costs more, about US$500, if you would rather not share, that a homestay with meals is approximately US$50 a day, and that transport costs can be reduced significantly by using the campus shuttle.

Living on campus or off campus

UWI Cave Hill has three Halls of Residence, offered on a first come, first served basis. Rooms are furnished single bedrooms with a shared kitchen, bathroom and living area, and you bring your own linen and kitchen things (UWI Travelling Information).

In the university's 2025/2026 Financial Information for International Students, a single room for Semester 1 was listed at US$1,512.50 in Frank Worrell Hall or Sherlock Hall, and US$2,722.50 in Keith Hunte Hall. There is also a US$150 advance deposit, which comes off your semester fees, and a US$100 security deposit. The same document lists off-campus options at about US$350 to US$450 a month. UWI's July 2026 pre-arrival guide says the 2026/2027 fees will be published shortly, so always check the latest figures before you commit.

If you live off campus, UWI keeps a list of approved accommodation and its Accommodations Assistant can help you search, but the agreement is between you and the landlord.

Tuition

Tuition depends on the school and the program. As two examples from the schools' own fee pages:

  • UWI Cave Hill (2025/2026, international students): most full-time undergraduate degrees in Humanities and Education, Social Sciences, and Science and Technology were US$18,650 a year. Law (LLB) was US$27,000, and the MBBS medical degree was US$32,500 a year for the pre-clinical years (UWI fee schedule).
  • Barbados Community College: the yearly total for extra-regional students is listed at BBD$8,925 (about US$4,463). That covers BBD$8,000 tuition, BBD$295 registration, BBD$330 medical insurance and the BBD$300 visa fee. Regional students pay a total of BBD$4,925. Some bachelor's programs cost more (BCC Fees).

Looking for a specific course? Our Program Finder lists programs across UWI Cave Hill, BCC, Codrington College and SJPI.

One-off costs to plan for

  • Student visa: the application fee is BBD$300, paid when you apply (Barbados Immigration Department).
  • Getting from the airport: UWI's pre-arrival guide estimates a taxi from Grantley Adams International Airport to the Cave Hill campus at US$47, with extra charges for additional luggage.
  • Setting up your room: halls do not supply linen or kitchen utensils, so budget for these in your first week.

Everyday costs worth knowing

  • Buses: a one-way trip is BBD$3.50, however far you travel (UWI pre-arrival guide).
  • Tax on purchases: the standard rate of VAT is 17.5%, and telecommunications services are taxed at 22% (Barbados Revenue Authority). The Revenue Authority also keeps a list of zero-rated items.
  • Health cover: fully registered UWI students are automatically enrolled in the Campus Medical Assistance Programme. It covers serious illness and accidents, but not dental, vision or preventative care, and it only applies within the Caribbean (UWI Travelling Information). BCC includes a BBD$330 medical insurance charge in its fees.

Why your budget matters for your visa

Your budget is not only for peace of mind. When you apply for a student visa, the Immigration Department asks for proof of financial support covering your school, living and medical expenses (Student Visa requirements). International students also cannot rely on a part-time job, which I explain in Can International Students Work in Barbados? So the numbers above are worth getting right before you apply.

Building your plan

Every student's budget is different. It depends on the school, the program, how you like to live and how often you want to travel home. If you would like help putting real numbers together for your family, book a free consultation and we will work through it with you. And when you are ready for the practical side of daily life, read Shopping, Getting Around and Things to Know.
