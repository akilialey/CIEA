Can International Students Work in Barbados?

What the rules say about working on a student visa, and the real options during and after your studies.

Patricia AtherleyFounder & Director, CIEA

13 November 2026·6 min read

Many students hope to pay part of their way with a part-time job, the way students often do in Canada, the UK or the US. Barbados works differently, and it is much better to know that before you arrive than after. Here is what the rules actually say, and what your options look like.

The short answer

In most cases, no. UWI Cave Hill's International Office puts it plainly: International students are not permitted to work in Barbados, except for limited hours on campus (UWI Travelling Information).

What the law says

The Barbados Immigration Department states that all non-nationals who want to work in Barbados must register with Immigration before they start any job, under Section 17 of the Immigration Act, CAP. 190 (Immigration Department, Work Permits). A student visa lets you live in Barbados to study. The student visa page does not give any right to work.

Work permits are applied for by the employer, not the student. A short-term permit lasts up to eleven months and a long-term permit up to three years. For a long-term permit, the employer has to show that no Barbadian or resident is capable and willing to do the job. The application fee is BBD$300, with a further fee once a permit is granted (Work Permits).

Plan your finances without a job

Because a part-time job is not an option for most international students, the student visa application asks for proof of financial support for your school, living and medical expenses (Student Visa requirements). Plan for the full cost of your studies up front. Our guide to what it costs to study and live in Barbados walks through the numbers.

On-campus work

UWI allows limited hours of work on campus. The details, such as which roles are open and how many hours you can do, are set by your school, so ask the International Office before you accept anything. You can reach UWI Cave Hill's International Office on (246) 417-4972 (UWI Travelling Information). If you are at another school, speak to its student services team first.

Internships and placements

If your program includes a placement with an organisation in Barbados, talk to your school early. Immigration's work permit page lists training attachments under short-term permits, with the same documents required but no final fee (Work Permits). Your school will know how this applies to your course.

If you are a CARICOM national

Students from CARICOM member states have an important extra route after graduation. Under the free movement of skills, a CARICOM national with a recognised qualification can work in other member states without a work permit by holding a Certificate of Recognition of CARICOM Skills Qualification (Barbados Accreditation Council).

  • Who qualifies: the eligible groups include graduates with at least a bachelor's degree from a recognised university, and holders of associate degrees and comparable qualifications, along with nurses, teachers, artisans and others (BAC guidelines booklet).
  • Where to apply in Barbados: the Barbados Accreditation Council issues the certificate. You apply in person, with documents including your birth certificate, passport copy, photos, qualifications and two professional references. There is a $100 application fee, and processing takes at least seven working days (BAC, CARICOM Skills Certificate).
  • Then Immigration: once you have the certificate, you return to the Immigration Department to be granted indefinite entry (BAC guidelines booklet).

Note that this right is tied to working. The Accreditation Council is clear that there is currently no free movement right purely to live in another member state.

After you graduate, if you are not from CARICOM

If you are not a CARICOM national and want to work in Barbados after your studies, you will need an employer to apply for a work permit for you, under the rules above. Rules can change, so always check the latest position with the Immigration Department and your school.

Making the most of your time

Not being able to take a part-time job does not mean you cannot build your future while you study. Many students use the time to focus fully on their grades, join clubs, and build the relationships with lecturers and classmates that lead to references and opportunities later. If you would like to talk through your plans, including your options after graduation, book a free consultation. You can also explore programs in our Program Finder or read our practical guide to student life.

This article is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Always confirm your own situation with the Barbados Immigration Department and your school.
